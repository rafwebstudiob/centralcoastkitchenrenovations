Kitchen renovation waste on the Central Coast

In short: Central Coast Council's bulk kerbside collection takes old appliances and some furniture, but not building materials such as tiles, plasterboard, timber or fibro. Those go to the Woy Woy or Buttonderry waste facilities for a fee, or in a skip arranged by you or your renovator. Asbestos loads must be double-wrapped and booked 24 hours ahead.

Who removes the old kitchen, and where it goes, is worth settling before you sign a quote. This page summarises Central Coast Council's published rules so you can compare quotes properly.

First, check what your quote includes

Many kitchen quotes include "demolition and rubbish removal", but not all do, and some exclude items such as old appliances or anything found to contain asbestos. Ask whether the renovator supplies a skip or takes the waste away, whether disposal fees are included, and what happens if fibro or asbestos is found behind the old kitchen.

Council's bulk kerbside collection: what it will and won't take

Central Coast Council says most households are entitled to six booked bulk kerbside collections a year, each no more than 2 m³ (roughly a standard box trailer). Entitlements reset on 1 February each year and belong to the property, so if a previous owner or tenant used them, they aren't available again until the next February.

Accepted in a general household collection include appliances, water heaters, fridges and freezers (tick the fridge box when you book so they're degassed), furniture, rugs and carpet.

Not accepted include building materials such as bricks, tiles, Gyprock, concrete and timber; glass or mirrors, including furniture with glass doors; paints and chemicals; and composite compressed boards such as fibro, fibre cement sheeting, Blue Board and Hardiplank. Council warns that if any of these fibre boards are detected, its contractor won't collect any of your bulk waste. Items must be under 1.8 m long and light enough for two people to lift, and should go out the night before your collection day, not earlier.

So an old fridge, oven or dishwasher can often go in a bulk collection, but the cabinet carcasses, benchtop offcuts, tiles and plasterboard from the strip-out generally can't.

Woy Woy and Buttonderry waste facilities

Council runs two waste management facilities: Woy Woy (Nagari Road, Woy Woy) and Buttonderry (850 Hue Hue Road, Jilliby). Council lists both as open 7am to 4pm Monday to Friday, 8am to 4pm Saturday and public holidays, and 8am to 2pm Sunday, closed Christmas Day, New Year's Day and Good Friday. The old Kincumber facility closed to the public in 2020.

  • Free: scrap metal (including whitegoods), household cardboard up to 200 kg, e-waste for residential customers, and some recyclables.
  • Fees apply: mixed waste, building and demolition waste, bricks, roof tiles, pavers and concrete, clean soil, and mattresses.
  • Paint: not accepted at Woy Woy. Household quantities go free to the Community Recycling Centre at Buttonderry, along with motor oil, gas bottles, batteries and fluoro tubes.

Asbestos in older Central Coast kitchens

Homes built before about 1990 may contain asbestos, for example in fibro sheeting or older floor coverings, and you can't tell by looking. SafeWork NSW says a licensed professional must remove any friable asbestos or more than 10 m² of bonded asbestos, and recommends a licensed professional for any amount.

If asbestos waste is going to a Council facility, Council says:

  • it's accepted at Woy Woy and Buttonderry only, and every load must be booked with the weighbridge at least 24 hours ahead
  • it must be double-wrapped in plastic at least 200 microns thick, as set out in Council's asbestos disposal guidelines
  • you tell the gate operator on arrival, and it won't be accepted in the last hour before closing
  • anyone moving 100 kg or more, or 10 m² or more of sheeting, must record the movement with the NSW EPA (a Movement ID for transporters, or the household asbestos form for householders moving their own).

When a skip makes sense

For a full kitchen strip-out, a skip often beats several trips to Jilliby or Woy Woy, because cabinets, benchtops, tiles and plasterboard all go in one place. Tell the skip company if there are tiles or a heavy stone benchtop, as heavy waste can have weight limits, and never put fibro or anything that might be asbestos in a general skip. If the skip has to sit on the road or footpath, check Central Coast Council's requirements first.

Questions to ask your kitchen renovator

  • Is removal and disposal of the old kitchen included in the price?
  • Will you supply a skip, and where will it go on my property?
  • What happens if asbestos is found, and who will remove it?
  • Will you take the old appliances, or should I book a bulk kerbside collection?
  • Can any cabinets or appliances in good condition be reused or donated instead?
